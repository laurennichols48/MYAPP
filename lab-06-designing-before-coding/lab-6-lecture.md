# Lab 6: Lecture

## Designing Before Coding

## 1. Design Communicates Priority

A design tells the visitor:

- What this page is.
- What matters most.
- What belongs together.
- What action is available next.

## 2. Visual Hierarchy

Visual hierarchy is the order your eye is drawn to things. Think of a restaurant menu, a street sign, or a movie poster: some things you notice instantly, others take a second look, and a few you'd only find if you went looking for them. That's not an accident — someone designed it that way.

Hierarchy can be created through:

- Size.
- Weight.
- Position.
- Contrast.
- Spacing.
- Grouping.

Using every signal at maximum strength removes hierarchy.

## 3. Contrast

Contrast helps distinguish elements, but it must support readability and purpose. Contrast can involve color, size, shape, spacing, or weight.

## 4. Alignment

Shared edges create order. Elements that appear related should usually align intentionally.

## 5. Repetition

Repeating navigation placement, card treatment, heading styles, and spacing makes a multi-page website feel coherent.

## 6. Whitespace

Whitespace is active design space. It separates groups, improves scanning, and prevents competing elements from merging visually.

## 7. Content Before Decoration

A content inventory identifies what must exist before deciding exactly how it looks.

## 8. Low-Fidelity Mockups

Low-fidelity mockups are fast and disposable. Their purpose is to test:

- Page regions.
- Reading order.
- Grouping.
- Approximate size.
- Responsive changes.

## 9. Responsive Design Is a Decision

A mobile design is not simply a smaller desktop design. Content may stack, spacing may change, and navigation may need a different arrangement.

## 10. Accessibility Is Part of Design

Readable contrast, meaningful order, visible focus, descriptive links, and adequate target size are design decisions, not final corrections.

## Check for Understanding

Ask students to identify:

1. The likely first focal point in a sample mockup.
2. Two elements that should align.
3. One section that should stack on mobile.
4. One design choice that could create an accessibility problem.
